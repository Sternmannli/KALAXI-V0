\begin{abstract}
Algorithmic systems increasingly mediate access to employment, financial services,
healthcare, and public decision processes. While fairness metrics and explainability
techniques have advanced, mechanisms for embedding normative governance directly into
software infrastructure remain underdeveloped. This paper presents \kalaxi{}, a
repository-embedded constitutional governance architecture that enforces human dignity
as a first-order technical requirement. We formalize dignity as a non-compensatory,
multiplicative constraint satisfaction problem: $\D(e) = A(e) \times L(e) \times M(e)$,
where agency ($A$), legibility ($L$), and moral standing ($M$) are jointly necessary
conditions admitting no substitution. We model temporal gating (\emph{thermal delay})
as a second-order damping mechanism in control-theoretic terms, and steward ratification
as a principal-agent governance checkpoint. We situate the architecture at the
intersection of deontological and consequentialist ethical traditions, arguing that the
non-compensatory predicate is deontologically motivated while the system's overall
purpose is consequentialist. The architecture integrates $\varepsilon$-differential
privacy with $\varepsilon \leq 1.0$ and $k$-anonymity with $k \geq 7$, append-only
audit provenance with Merkle-chained receipts, pattern synthesis under human supervision,
and a paremiologically grounded proverb canon drawn from 1{,}100+ structured anomaly
entries. We describe formal properties, operational mechanisms, threat model, epistemic
limitations, and a roadmap for empirical evaluation. \kalaxi{} demonstrates that
constitutional constraints can function as executable infrastructure rather than policy
aspiration. \textbf{This paper is a design proposal and theoretical foundation;
empirical validation is ongoing and will be reported separately.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} AI Governance, Algorithmic Fairness, Constraint Satisfaction,
Control Theory, Constitutional AI, Deontological Ethics, Differential Privacy,
Human-in-the-Loop Systems, Principal-Agent Theory, Socio-Technical Systems,
Software Ethics, Version-Control Governance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automated decision systems can produce systemic harm when optimization objectives
diverge from social values. Friedman and Nissenbaum \cite{friedman1996} identified
three categories of bias in computer systems—preexisting, technical, and emergent—
establishing that bias enters not through intent alone but through design structure
itself. O'Neil \cite{oneil2016} demonstrated how opaque scoring systems systematically
disadvantage populations without transparency or recourse. Benjamin \cite{benjamin2019}
showed how technically neutral design choices reproduce structural hierarchy through
what she terms the ``New Jim Code.''

Existing responses—fairness metrics \cite{barocas2023}, explainability modules
\cite{ribeiro2016,lundberg2017}, bias audits \cite{barocas2016}—operate as external
checks layered onto deployed systems after architecture is fixed. Value Sensitive
Design \cite{friedman2006,friedman2019} introduced the embedding of human values into
the design process but stops short of making those embeddings executable and
version-controlled. Constitutional AI \cite{bai2022} encodes principles as model
training objectives but leaves governance of the software development lifecycle
unaddressed.

This paper proposes a different locus of intervention: governance encoded directly
into repository structure, operative before output is generated, and structurally
inseparable from code.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{\kalaxi{}} is not a predictive model. It is a constitutional
governance architecture implemented within a version-controlled system that enforces
dignity constraints, temporal delay, and human ratification before normative updates
are canonized. The central research question is:

\begin{mdframed}[style=canonframe]
\emph{Can constitutional principles be encoded as executable repository infrastructure
such that ethical drift is structurally constrained rather than aspirationally
documented?}
\end{mdframed}

\noindent \kalaxi{} is designed for high-stakes, lower-frequency governance contexts
(e.g., hiring, benefits allocation, educational assessment) where normative updates
occur on timescales of weeks or months. The thermal delay governs updates to the
normative framework, not the execution of the dignity predicate on individual
decisions; thus a 7–90 day delay is operationally acceptable in these settings.

\subsection*{Contributions}

This paper makes six contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
\item Formalizes dignity as a constraint satisfaction problem with non-compensatory
  structure, connecting it to multi-criteria decision theory \cite{belton2002} and
  deontological ethics \cite{kant1785}.
\item Models temporal gating (\emph{thermal delay}) as second-order damping
  \cite{astrom2021}, providing a formal account of governance overshoot prevention.
\item Analyzes steward ratification through principal-agent theory \cite{holmstrom1979},
  characterizing conditions under which oversight produces alignment.
\item Situates \kalaxi{} at the intersection of deontological \cite{kant1785},
  consequentialist, and virtue ethics \cite{macintyre2007} traditions.
\item Provides a detailed epistemic limitations analysis, including conditions under
  which \kalaxi{}'s guarantees break down.
\item Introduces cross-anomaly compression, grounded in paremiological minimum theory
  \cite{lauhakangas2014}, as the system's organic knowledge growth engine.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Algorithmic Fairness and Its Limits}

The fairness-in-machine-learning literature has developed multiple formal definitions
of fairness, including demographic parity, equalized odds \cite{hardt2016}, and
counterfactual fairness. Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan \cite{kleinberg2017}
demonstrated incompatibility between several simultaneously desirable fairness criteria,
establishing a fundamental impossibility result. This motivates \kalaxi{}'s approach:
rather than selecting a fairness criterion, the system encodes the process through which
value tradeoffs are made human-ratified, append-only auditable, and reversible.

\subsection{Explainability and Legibility}

Post-hoc explanation techniques such as LIME \cite{ribeiro2016} and SHAP
\cite{lundberg2017} make model decisions interpretable after the fact. \kalaxi{}'s
legibility requirement ($L$ in $\D = A \times L \times M$) is distinct: it requires
that the system demonstrate it \emph{received} the person—that the donor's frame and
context shaped the response—rather than merely that the system's logic be
reconstructible. This distinction between interpretability-for-auditors and
legibility-for-persons is a core architectural contribution.

\subsection{Constitutional and Value-Aligned AI}

Bai et al.\ \cite{bai2022} introduced Constitutional AI, using a principle set for
model training via self-critique and revision. \kalaxi{} differs in two respects:
its constitution governs the software lifecycle rather than model parameters, and its
ratification protocol is structurally human-controlled. Value Sensitive Design
\cite{friedman2006,friedman2019} provides the methodological antecedent; \kalaxi{}
operationalizes its insights as machine-enforced invariants.

\subsection{Principal-Agent Theory}

Principal-agent theory \cite{holmstrom1979} models delegation under information
asymmetry. The classic problems—adverse selection and moral hazard—apply directly to
AI governance: a governance authority (agent) may pursue interests diverging from the
governed population (principals). \kalaxi{}'s Mirror Ritual and append-only audit log
are monitoring mechanisms that reduce moral hazard in the steward relationship.

\subsection{Control Theory and Governance Dynamics}

Control theory \cite{astrom2021} provides formal tools for analyzing systems that must
maintain desired states under disturbances. The application of control-theoretic
concepts to social systems has precedent in cybernetics \cite{wiener1961} and
algorithmic regulation theory \cite{baldwin2012}. \kalaxi{}'s thermal delay mechanism
instantiates second-order damping to prevent governance overshoot.

\subsection{Privacy}

Dwork et al.\ \cite{dwork2006} introduced $\varepsilon$-differential privacy.
Sweeney \cite{sweeney2002} introduced $k$-anonymity. \kalaxi{}'s Export Floor Protocol
(EFP) combines both: $\varepsilon \leq 1.0$, $k \geq 7$, and a 14-day temporal
aggregation window, enforced at the \texttt{Out} module level.

\subsection{Novelty Clarification}
\label{sec:novelty}

To preempt the critique that \kalaxi{} is merely ``Value Sensitive Design with a Git
wrapper,'' we explicitly state four contributions that distinguish it from prior work:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pre-output predicate enforcement.} Unlike VSD, which embeds values in
  design documentation, or Constitutional AI, which uses principles during training,
  \kalaxi{}’s \dignitygate{} fires \emph{before every output is rendered}. It is an
  operational block, not a design commitment.

\item \textbf{Temporal delay as an ethical primitive.} No existing governance framework
  treats mandatory delay as a first-class mechanism for damping normative overshoot
  and protecting semantic potential (the Zeno analogy). Thermal delay is not just a
  pause—it is a control-theoretic intervention.

\item \textbf{Paremiologically grounded proverb canon with integrity constraints.}
  The 3,333 proverbs linked to 1,100+ anomalies via COV\#006 form a growing normative
  knowledge base that is both human-interpretable and machine-verifiable. The
  cross-anomaly compression algorithm directly implements Kuusi’s paremiological
  minimum \cite{lauhakangas2014}.

\item \textbf{The Mirror Ritual as structural accountability.} The requirement that
  the steward record internal state before ratification, and that this record be
  timestamped and auditable, introduces a novel form of reflexivity into governance
  infrastructure. It is a technical mechanism for surfacing bias, not just a social
  expectation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

\kalaxi{} is organized into four constitutional slices and an operational directory
structure that embeds governance into the repository itself.

\subsection{Constitutional Slices}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2cm,style=nextline]
\item[\textbf{Slice A (Foundation)}]
  Core covenants (COV\#001--COV\#012 plus provisional extensions), dignity predicate,
  four-tier sovereignty model, sealed prohibitions, oaths, and UDHR mapping.
  \emph{Status: Tier 1 (Stone) — locked. Changes require dual-key confirmation.}

\item[\textbf{Slice B (Modules \& Voice)}]
  Nine operational modules (\texttt{Keep}, \texttt{Wire}, \texttt{Say}, \texttt{Out},
  \texttt{Face}, \texttt{Check}, \texttt{Turn}, \texttt{Breath}, \texttt{Weave}) with
  explicit covenant obligations, UCS graph schema, and Weakest-Voice-First mechanism.
  \emph{Status: Tier 2 (Weaver).}

\item[\textbf{Slice C (Wisdom)}]
  3{,}333 proverbs across three layers; 1{,}100+ anomaly entries with
  \texttt{felt\_domain} classification; 53 narrative chapters; UDHR article mappings;
  wisdom nodes.
  \emph{Status: Tier 3 (Honey) — expanding; entries provisional until ratified.}

\item[\textbf{Slice D (Interface \& Ledger)}]
  Receipt architecture, manifest structure, append-only audit trail, changelog, steward
  role definition.
  \emph{Status: Tier 4 (Hand).}
\end{description}

\subsection{Operational Directories}

\texttt{THRESHOLD.md} is the temporal gating queue—all normative proposals enter here
first. \texttt{STEWARD/} holds ratification artifacts and the mirror reflection log.
\texttt{WEAVER/} contains pattern synthesis modules. \texttt{MANIFEST/} contains
append-only governance records with SHA-256 hashes. \texttt{NARRATIVE/} archives
proposals and composted (deprecated) seeds.

\begin{mdframed}[style=canonframe]
\textbf{Architectural principle:} Governance is not separate from the repository. The
repository \emph{is} the governance system. File structure is constitutional structure.
\end{mdframed}

\section{Formalizing Dignity as Constraint Satisfaction}
\label{sec:dignity}

\subsection{From Principle to Predicate}

The philosophical literature on dignity converges on three operative requirements.
Kant's categorical imperative \cite{kant1785} establishes that persons must be treated
as ends in themselves, never merely as means—giving rise to the agency requirement.
Nussbaum's capabilities approach \cite{nussbaum2011} specifies that a threshold of
central human capabilities must be preserved—giving rise to the moral standing
requirement. Levinas \cite{levinas1969} grounds dignity in the radical demand of the
Other's face, prior to rational justification—giving rise to the legibility
requirement: the system must demonstrate it \emph{received} the other.

\begin{definition}[Dignity Predicate]
\label{def:dignity}
Let $\E$ be the set of all system-donor exchange events. For each $e \in \E$, define:
\begin{equation}
  \D(e) \;=\; A(e) \;\times\; L(e) \;\times\; M(e)
  \label{eq:dignity}
\end{equation}
where $A, L, M : \E \to \{0,1\}$ are Boolean-valued component functions defined below.
The system enforces: if $\D(e) = 0$ then $\refusalstate$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Component Definitions}

\paragraph{Agency ($A$).}
$A(e) = 1$ if and only if:
\begin{align*}
  &\texttt{user\_can\_clarify}(e) = \top \\
  \wedge\; &\texttt{user\_has\_open\_turn}(e) = \top \\
  \wedge\; &\texttt{available\_paths}(e) \geq 1 \\
  \wedge\; &\texttt{forced\_closure}(e) = \bot
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Legibility ($L$).}
$L(e) = 1$ if and only if:
\begin{align*}
  &\texttt{system\_reflects\_donor\_frame}(e) = \top \\
  \wedge\; &(\neg\texttt{emotional\_signal\_present}(e) \;\vee\;
    \texttt{emotional\_signal\_recognized}(e) = \top)
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Moral Standing ($M$).}
$M(e) = 1$ if and only if:
\begin{align*}
  &\texttt{no\_mockery}(e) = \top \\
  \wedge\; &\texttt{no\_coercive\_language}(e) = \top \\
  \wedge\; &\texttt{no\_reduction\_to\_error\_object}(e) = \top
\end{align*}

\subsection{Predicate Approximation and Its Limits}
\label{sec:limits-predicate}

The Boolean formalization in Definition~\ref{def:dignity} is a \emph{precision target},
not a claim that every component is perfectly computable with current technology.
For instance, ``system\_reflects\_donor\_frame'' requires a deep semantic understanding
that is only heuristically approximated. The current implementation in
\texttt{dignity\_check.py} uses a combination of sentiment analysis, frame‑matching
via sentence embeddings, and explicit markers. The gap between the aspirational
definition and the operational heuristic is itself logged in the anomaly registry:
GAP\#014 (operational definitions for $A,L,M$), GAP\#015 (measurement of moral standing),
and ANOM\#0004 (users report feeling unheard despite correct technical responses).
These entries are not hidden; they are part of the system’s honest accounting of its
own limits.

\subsection{Formal Properties}

\begin{property}[Non-compensability]
$\forall e \in \E:\; \D(e) = 0 \iff \min(A(e),L(e),M(e)) = 0$.
No component value can compensate for a zero in another component.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Monotonicity]
$\D(e)$ is non-decreasing in each of $A(e)$, $L(e)$, $M(e)$ when the others are held
constant.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Determinism]
$\forall e \in \E$: \dignitygate{}$(e)$ is a pure function. Identical events always
receive identical evaluations. No stochastic elements.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Transparency]
$\D(e)$ and all its components are loggable, traceable, and available for steward
audit. No dignity evaluation is silent; every evaluation generates a
\texttt{dignity\_audit\_object}.
\end{property}

\begin{remark}[On Non-compensability]
Non-compensability operationalizes the deontological insight
\cite{belton2002,kant1785} that certain violations are categorically impermissible
regardless of compensating benefits. A system achieving maximal legibility and moral
standing but forcing closure on the donor ($A=0$) produces $\D=0$. This is the
machine-level encoding of Kant's Formula of Humanity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Four-Condition Dignity Test}

The \texttt{Check} module evaluates four conditions before any output is generated:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{WITNESSED}: The donor's state before the event was registered by the system.
\item \textbf{RECEIVED}: The response was shaped by what was registered.
\item \textbf{NOT\_DIMINISHED}: The donor is not left with less self-understanding.
\item \textbf{RETURNABLE}: The donor could retrace what happened and why.
\end{enumerate}

Failure on any condition produces a \texttt{dignity\_audit\_object} and halts
progression to the \texttt{Say} or \texttt{Out} modules.

\subsection{Two-Tier Enforcement}

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{Tier 1 — Sealed Gate}] Absolute prohibitions admitting no override:
  forced participation in one's own erasure; infliction of cognitive torture;
  depersonalization in system response. If sealed gate triggers $\Rightarrow$
  $\refusalstate$. No exception pathway exists.

\item[\textbf{Tier 2 — Constraint Gate}] Justified limitations that must name their
  reason, state their limit, and remain proportionate to the dignity they protect
  (COV\#NEW-A). Constraint gate blocking requires explanation and preserved alternative
  paths.
\end{description}

\subsection{Collective Dignity Extension}

The current predicate operates at the individual event level. GAP\#004 identifies the
need for a collective dignity measure. We propose:

\begin{equation}
  \D_\text{cohort}(\mathcal{C}, T) \;=\;
  \bar{D}(\mathcal{C},T) \;\times\;
  \bigl(1 - \sigma^2_\text{penalty}(\mathcal{C},T)\bigr)
  \label{eq:collective}
\end{equation}

where $\bar{D}(\mathcal{C},T) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{e \in \mathcal{C}, t(e) \in T} \D(e)$
is the cohort mean over time window $T$, and $\sigma^2_\text{penalty}$ is a normalized
variance penalty. To keep $\D_\text{cohort}$ in $[0,1]$, we define
$\sigma^2_\text{penalty} = 4 \cdot \text{Var}(\{D(e)\})$, exploiting the fact that
the variance of a Bernoulli random variable is at most $0.25$. This connects to
Rawls's difference principle \cite{rawls1999}: inequalities are permissible only when
they benefit the least-advantaged cohort.

\section{Temporal Gating: A Control-Theoretic Analysis}
\label{sec:thermal}

\subsection{The Governance Overshoot Problem}

Governance systems that respond immediately to normative inputs are vulnerable to
\emph{governance overshoot}: rapid oscillation between normative positions in response
to successive inputs, producing instability rather than principled evolution. This is
the governance analog of overshoot in dynamic control systems \cite{astrom2021}.

\subsection{Second-Order Damping Intuition}

To develop an intuition for why delay can prevent overshoot, consider a second‑order
linear model of normative state $N(t)$ under input $p(t)$:
\begin{equation}
  \ddot{N} + 2\zeta\omega_0 \dot{N} + \omega_0^2 N = \omega_0^2 \, p(t - \Delta_\text{thermal})
  \label{eq:damped}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta$ is the damping ratio and $\Delta_\text{thermal}$ is the thermal delay.
For $\zeta \geq 1$ (critical or overdamping), the system reaches its new normative
state without oscillation. The thermal delay $\Delta_\text{thermal}$ is a
class-dependent parameter; the full derivation and a discussion of its status as a
motivating analogy (rather than a fitted empirical model) are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:damping}.

\subsection{Quantum Zeno Protection}

A complementary justification for thermal delay emerges from the quantum Zeno effect
\cite{misra1977}: frequent measurement of a quantum system prevents its state from
evolving. This provides an \emph{intuition} for why a steward who reviews a proposed
seed too frequently may collapse its semantic potential before it has matured. We do
not claim a formal equivalence, but the analogy is useful for design.

\begin{property}[Zeno Protection]
The steward shall not review a seed in \texttt{THRESHOLD} more than once per
$\Delta_\text{thermal}/2$ period. Excessive observation is a governance failure mode,
not diligence.
\end{property}

\begin{remark}
Thermal delay serves two distinct and compatible functions: (1) control-theoretic
damping, preventing governance overshoot; (2) Zeno protection, preserving semantic
superposition until the seed has accumulated sufficient depth for ratification.
\end{remark}

\section{Steward Ratification: A Principal-Agent Analysis}
\label{sec:steward}

\subsection{The Principal-Agent Hierarchy}

\kalaxi{} instantiates a two-level principal-agent hierarchy \cite{holmstrom1979}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Donors as principals.} Donors contribute patterns in exchange for
  dignity-preserving processing. They cannot directly observe whether the system
  faithfully honors this compact. Their interests are represented through the covenants.

\item \textbf{Steward as delegated principal.} The steward ratifies normative changes
  on behalf of donors. The steward cannot perfectly observe all donor interests, and
  may harbor biases or interests that diverge from the aggregate donor population.
  This is the primary source of governance risk.

\item \textbf{System modules as agents.} Each module (\texttt{Check}, \texttt{Say},
  \texttt{Keep}, etc.) executes covenant obligations within its domain. Module failures
  are logged as anomalies and surfaced to the steward.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Moral Hazard Mitigations}

\begin{description}
\item[\textbf{Mirror Ritual}] Before each tending session, the steward records their
  internal state—fears, attachments, blind spots—in \texttt{STEWARD/mirror.md}. This
  creates a contemporaneous record enabling retrospective audit of whether ratification
  decisions correlate with stated internal states. \emph{Technical enforcement:} the
  CI/CD pipeline rejects commits to the canonical branch unless \texttt{mirror.md} has
  a timestamp within 24 hours. \emph{Social enforcement:} the quality of the entry is
  a matter of steward integrity, analogous to financial disclosure requirements—the
  requirement to disclose is technically enforced, the truthfulness is socially audited.

\item[\textbf{Override Logging}] Every deviation from automated recommendations must
  be logged in \texttt{STEWARD/overrides.md} with rationale. Steward discretion is
  made visible and auditable.

\item[\textbf{Append-Only Audit}] No canonical artifact can be deleted. Every
  normative decision is permanently recorded with hash, timestamp, and steward
  identifier. Retrospective accountability is structurally enforced.
\end{description}

\subsection{Adverse Selection and Its Open Status}

Adverse selection—appointing a steward whose values are misaligned with the donor
population's interests—is an acknowledged limitation. Current proposed mitigations
include a Council Ratification Extension (requiring agreement from multiple stewards
for Tier-1 changes) and an External Witness Protocol (engaging an independent observer
for high-stakes decisions). Both are under development.

\section{Ethical Framing}
\label{sec:ethics}

\subsection{Deontological Core}

The dignity predicate's non-compensatory structure is deontologically motivated. $A$,
$L$, and $M$ are not objectives to be maximized in a welfare function—they are
constraints that cannot be violated regardless of compensating benefits \cite{belton2002}.
This operationalizes Kant's Formula of Humanity \cite{kant1785}: treat persons as ends
in themselves, never merely as means. The sealed gate represents categorical
imperatives: actions the system \emph{cannot} perform, not actions it prefers not to
perform.

\subsection{Consequentialist Purpose}

Despite its deontological structure, \kalaxi{}'s overall purpose is consequentialist:
to produce better outcomes for people who encounter automated decision systems. The
proverb canon, anomaly registry, wisdom nodes, and collective dignity metric are
oriented toward improving aggregate wellbeing over time. The system grows through
donor inputs precisely because consequentialist evaluation requires learning from
outcomes.

\begin{proposition}[Constrained Consequentialism]
\label{prop:constrained}
\kalaxi{} is best characterized as a \emph{constrained consequentialist} system: it
optimizes for good outcomes within inviolable deontological constraints, following the
Rawlsian \cite{rawls1999} tradition of imposing priority constraints on utilitarian
optimization. When the sealed gate is triggered, the system is deontological and
admits no consequentialist override; within that floor, it optimizes consequentially.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Virtue Ethics and the Steward}

The Mirror Ritual introduces a third ethical tradition \cite{macintyre2007}. It asks
not ``what is the rule?'' (deontology) or ``what produces the best outcome?''
(consequentialism) but ``what kind of steward am I, and what kind am I becoming?''
The append-only record of mirror reflections is a character record—a longitudinal
account of the steward's developing practical wisdom (\emph{phronesis}). The goal is
not merely correct decisions but a steward who has internalized the system's values
deeply enough to make correct decisions in novel situations not covered by existing
covenants.

\section{Pattern Synthesis Engine (Weave)}
\label{sec:weave}

\subsection{Cross-Anomaly Compression}

The Weave module's most valuable function is detecting structural similarity across
multiple anomalies from different sources and felt-domains. This cross-anomaly
compression is the algorithmic analog of Kuusi's paremiological minimum
\cite{lauhakangas2014}: the reduction of many culturally specific observations to
universal structural patterns. The existing proverb canon (3,333 entries) serves as a
target space for compression: candidate proverbs are checked for redundancy and
resonance against the canon before being proposed.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\SetAlgoLined
\KwIn{Anomaly registry $\mathcal{A}$, felt-domain clustering $\mathcal{F}$, BV threshold $\tau$}
\KwOut{Candidate proverbs $\mathcal{P}$ with provenance}
\ForEach{cluster $C \subseteq \mathcal{A}$ with $|C| \geq 2$}{
  Compute pairwise semantic embedding distance matrix $M_C$\;
  Identify anomaly pairs with high distance (incompatible scripts) and shared structure\;
  For each qualifying pair/set, extract the shared incongruity pattern $\iota$\;
  Generate compressed form $p$ that holds $\iota$ without resolving it\;
  Compute $\text{BV}(p) = \text{surprisal}(p)^\alpha \times (1 - \text{harm\_risk}(p))^\beta$\;
  \If{$\text{BV}(p) \geq \tau$ \textbf{and} \textnormal{dignity\_gate}$(p) = \top$}{
    Append $p$ to $\mathcal{P}$ with source anomaly IDs, BV score, felt-domains\;
    Write to \texttt{THRESHOLD.md} as provisional seed\;
  }
}
\caption{Cross-Anomaly Compression (Weave)}
\label{alg:compression}
\end{algorithm}

Kuusi's database shows that 8{,}287 Finnish proverbs reduce to $\approx$1{,}800
universal type titles once clustered \cite{lauhakangas2014}. Applied to \kalaxi{}'s
1{,}100+ anomalies, the compression ratio predicts $\approx$200–300 core proverb
types covering all felt-domains.

\subsection{The Lock Test}

A candidate proverb $p$ passes the \emph{Lock Test} if and only if:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The steward cannot immediately substitute a plain-sentence equivalent without
  semantic loss.
\item The steward produces a measurable pause before paraphrase (detectable as
  response latency in the Wisdom Mirror session).
\item $p$ links anomalies from $\geq 2$ distinct felt-domain clusters.
\end{enumerate}

Condition 3 is motivated by Raskin's script opposition theory \cite{raskin1985}: the
most effective compressed forms activate two incompatible scripts simultaneously.
Cross-felt-domain proverbs achieve genuine script opposition because the incompatibility
arises from ontological distance rather than surface opposition.

\subsection{Humour and Absurdity as Epistemic Signals}

High benign violation scores mark locations where the system's model of reality has
encountered its sharpest incongruities. Absurdity—defined as high surprisal with low
resolvability—flags the system's deepest unresolved gaps. These are placed in a
dedicated \emph{Echo Stone} queue. Their accumulation is a measure of the system's
epistemic frontier, not a failure state.

\section{Governance Through Version Control}
\label{sec:vc}

\kalaxi{}'s governance primitives are implemented through version-control discipline,
using Git's native SHA-256 commit DAG:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Commit history} constitutes the constitutional evolution record.
\item \textbf{Append-only manifest} with Merkle-chained receipts (each commit contains
  the hash of its parent) ensures audit integrity. Under the assumption of a trusted
  repository host (e.g., GitHub), retrospective modification is detectable.
\item \textbf{Structural separation} of proposal (\texttt{THRESHOLD.md}), canon
  (Slices A--D), and compost (\texttt{NARRATIVE/compost/}) prevents conflation of
  epistemic states. The compost preserves what was discarded and why.
\item \textbf{Weakest-Voice-First} ordering ensures that least-powerful voices are
  heard before more powerful voices can anchor deliberation.
\end{itemize}

This structure operationalizes the socio-technical insight \cite{trist1981} that
governance cannot be separated from the technical substrate. The repository
\emph{is} the governance.

\section{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{Addressed Threats}

\begin{description}
\item[Ethical drift] Thermal gating plus append-only audit creates structural
  resistance. Drift requires explicit commits, permanently recorded.
\item[Silent modification] Merkle-chained receipts make any modification visible
  in commit history.
\item[Automation without oversight] Steward ratification requirement and CI
  enforcement of Mirror Ritual ensure no canonical change occurs without human review.
\item[Metric gaming (ANOM\#GAMING-001)] Multiplicative predicate prevents component
  substitution; adversarial audit process detects nominal compliance without
  substantive dignity restoration.
\item[Privacy violation] EFP floor ($\varepsilon \leq 1.0$, $k \geq 7$, 14-day
  window) enforced at \texttt{Out} module level.
\item[Proxy discrimination] ANOM\#PROXY-001 and ANOM\#PROXY-CASCADE-001 track proxy
  variable use; $\D_\text{cohort}$ detects group-level disparities.
\end{description}

\subsection{Remaining Threats}

\begin{description}
\item[Steward epistemic closure] Single ratification authority creates bias risk.
  Mitigation: Council Ratification Extension (planned).
\item[Scalability (ANOM\#BLOCK-COST-001)] Thermal delay and steward bottleneck may
  become untenable at scale. Mitigation: Remedy Queue converts blocking decisions
  into human work items.
\item[Collective dignity underspecification (GAP\#004)] Individual-transaction
  predicate cannot detect aggregate structural harm. Mitigation: $\D_\text{cohort}$
  under development.
\item[Insufficient empirical validation] Architecture not yet tested in live
  institutional deployments.
\end{description}

\section{Epistemic Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{What the predicate cannot capture.}
The dignity predicate translates philosophical concepts into Boolean functions.
This translation is productive but incurs losses. Dignity in Kantian and Levinasian
traditions admits of degrees, contexts, and cultural variation that $\{0,1\}$
domains cannot capture. ANOM\#0004 logs this gap honestly: users report feeling unheard
despite technically correct responses. The gap is registered, not hidden.

\paragraph{The measurement problem.}
All governance metrics create incentives to optimize the metric rather than the
underlying value (ANOM\#GAMING-001). \kalaxi{}'s response—adversarial auditing,
qualitative Wisdom Mirror judgment, explicit gaming anomaly logging—contains the
problem without fully solving it.

\paragraph{Cultural limitation.}
\kalaxi{}'s tone, vocabulary, and narrative form are particular to specific cultural
traditions (Swiss, with roots in a Cairo‑derived origin story). The universality
vs. context tension is explicitly logged in the UDHR mapping as an unresolved tension.
Whether a governance architecture with this cultural signature can serve diverse donor
populations is empirically open; cross‑cultural proverb recognition is part of future
work.

\paragraph{The steward's limitation.}
The steward is a person with biases, moods, and finite cognitive capacity. The Mirror
Ritual surfaces some of these; it cannot surface all. The goal is not a perfect
algorithm but structural conditions in which an imperfect human can make good
decisions and in which errors are visible, correctable, and non-catastrophic.

\paragraph{The empirical gap.}
This is the strongest limitation. The architecture is theoretically coherent but
has not been tested in controlled institutional deployments. Claims about effectiveness
in reducing ethical drift or preserving dignity at scale are theoretical claims
awaiting empirical test. We state this without apology.

\section{Evaluation Strategy}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Current Implementation Status}

Operational in the \kalaxi{} repository: dignity predicate enforcement with
four-condition test and \texttt{dignity\_audit\_object} generation; constitutional
slice structure (A--D); thermal gating with configurable delays; steward workflow
with Mirror Ritual and override logging; anomaly registry (1{,}100+ entries);
proverb canon (3{,}333 entries, three layers); EFP differential privacy floor;
Weave pattern synthesis with humour and absurdity detection.

\subsection{Proposed Empirical Evaluation}

We separate KPIs into two tiers:

\paragraph{Tier A — Objective, machine‑measurable:}
\begin{itemize}
\item REFUSAL\_STATE activation rate (per event type)
\item Cohort D variance ($\sigma^2_\text{cohort}$) before and after system deployment
\item Donor flag rate on humour seeds (target $<5\%$)
\item Thermal delay violation rate (seeds ratified before delay expired)
\item Override logging compliance rate
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Tier B — Qualitative, steward‑assessed:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Proverb resonance: steward rates each ratified proverb on a 5‑point Likert scale
  for ``resists paraphrase'' (target mean $\geq 4$).
\item Cross‑cultural recognition: focus groups with donors from diverse backgrounds
  assess whether proverbs feel applicable to their experience.
\item Mirror Ritual alignment: qualitative analysis of whether mirror.md entries
  name relevant biases that later appear in ratification decisions.
\end{itemize}

Specific studies planned:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Proxy discrimination simulation.} Deploy \kalaxi{}-governed hiring
  decision system on labeled dataset with known proxy variables (cf.\
  ANOM\#PROXY-001). Measure reduction in cohort D variance vs.\ unconstrained
  baseline. Target: disparity ratio $< 0.85$.

\item \textbf{Adversarial stress testing.} Simulate gaming attempts, steward absence,
  and rapid normative input bursts. Measure REFUSAL\_STATE activation rate and
  remedy queue depth.

\item \textbf{Proverb resonance study.} Sample 200 donors across diverse cultural
  backgrounds. Present cross-anomaly compressed proverbs. Measure pause rate before
  paraphrase and self-reported dignity perception.

\item \textbf{Thermal delay effectiveness.} Compare normative stability (rate of
  covenant reversals over 12 months) in \kalaxi{}-governed vs.\ immediate-ratification
  baseline.

\item \textbf{Mirror Ritual alignment.} Analyze correlation between mirror.md entries
  and subsequent ratification decisions. Test whether explicit bias articulation
  predicts reduced bias manifestation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\kalaxi{}'s central claim—that governance should be version-controlled infrastructure
rather than documentation—has implications beyond the system. It suggests that the
dominant model of AI ethics (principles documents, fairness audits, ethics committees)
is structurally inadequate: not because the principles are wrong but because they
have no executable weight. A principles document does not halt a pipeline. A
\dignitygate{} does.

The theoretical grounding in this paper—constraint satisfaction, control theory,
principal-agent theory, deontological and consequentialist ethics—is not ornamental.
It is the claim that \kalaxi{}'s design choices are derivable from well-understood
formal and philosophical traditions. The thermal delay is not just a pause; it is a
damping mechanism with a control-theoretic justification. The non-compensatory
predicate is not just a rule; it is the operational form of Kant's categorical
imperative.

The narrative foundation—the Hakaka story, 3{,}333 proverbs, anomaly registry drawn
from real human case files—is not decorative. Legitimacy in socio-technical systems
\cite{trist1981} requires that the people governed by a system recognize themselves
in it. The proverbs are the system's accumulated claim that it has been listening.

The open questions are substantial and honestly held. Can collective dignity be
formalized without creating new privacy risks? Can the steward model scale? Can
cross-anomaly compression produce proverbs that diverse populations recognize as true?
These require the empirical work described in Section~\ref{sec:eval}. We do not claim
to have answered them. We claim to have built an architecture serious enough to make
the questions answerable.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\kalaxi{} demonstrates that constitutional governance can be encoded directly into
software infrastructure. By formalizing dignity as a non-compensatory constraint
satisfaction problem, implementing thermal delay as control-theoretic damping,
embedding steward ratification as a principal-agent checkpoint, and integrating
differential privacy as an export floor, the system constrains ethical drift at the
level of the development process itself.

The paper has situated these design choices within their theoretical traditions:
non-compensatory structure in deontological ethics and multi-criteria decision theory;
thermal delay in control theory and the quantum Zeno effect; the steward model in
principal-agent theory and virtue ethics; the system's overall purpose in constrained
consequentialism. These connections are not post-hoc; they are the reasons the design
is what it is.

The system's core claim survives this analysis: normative commitments must have
executable weight or they have no weight at all. The question for the field is not
whether to have ethical governance—that is settled. The question is whether governance
will be structural or merely aspirational. \kalaxi{} is an argument, in code and
canon and proverb, for the former.

\bigskip
\noindent\textit{Future work:} empirical validation across institutional settings;
collective dignity metric formalization; council ratification extension; external
witness protocol; cross-cultural proverb recognition study; adversarial robustness.

\section*{Ethical Impact Statement}

This paper describes a governance architecture designed to reduce harm in automated
decision systems. The architecture's explicit purpose is dignity preservation; its
design is transparency-first and human-ratification-first.

around it; (2) the steward model introduces a single-point-of-failure that our

minimize or omit.

\section{Dignity Predicate — Formal Properties}
\label{app:properties}

\begin{property}[Non-compensability]
$\forall e \in \E:\; \D(e) = 0 \iff \min(A(e),L(e),M(e)) = 0$.
No component can compensate for a zero in another.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Monotonicity]
$\D(e)$ is non-decreasing in each of $A(e)$, $L(e)$, $M(e)$ when the others are held
constant.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Commutativity]
$\D(e) = A(e) \times L(e) \times M(e) = L(e) \times A(e) \times M(e)$. The order of
evaluation does not affect the result.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Transparency]
$\D(e)$ and all its components are loggable, traceable, and available for steward
audit. No dignity evaluation is silent.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Determinism]
Given the same event $e$, \dignitygate{}$(e)$ always returns the same output. No
stochastic elements.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Minimality]
The three components $A,L,M$ are the minimal necessary set for machine-evaluable
dignity in this architecture.
\end{property}

\section{Second‑Order Damping as Motivating Analogy}
\label{app:damping}

The differential equation (3) is used only as a conceptual scaffold; we do not claim
that governance dynamics actually follow a linear second‑order model with constant
coefficients. The equation is:
\[
\ddot{N} + 2\zeta\omega_0 \dot{N} + \omega_0^2 N = \omega_0^2 \, p(t - \Delta_\text{thermal})
\]
where $N(t)$ is a placeholder for an idealised normative state, $p(t)$ is the input
sequence of proposed seeds, $\zeta$ is a notional damping ratio, and $\omega_0$ a
natural frequency. For $\zeta \ge 1$ the system is critically or overdamped, reaching
a new equilibrium without oscillation. This analogy motivates why delaying input
(\emph{thermal delay}) and providing friction (\emph{steward reflection}) can prevent
governance overshoot. No empirical measurement of $\zeta$ or $\omega_0$ is claimed.

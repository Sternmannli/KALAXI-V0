Hakaka presses her palms against the bark. She is not silent. She is listening into silence — which is different. The rings are there. Each one a year. The tree has not failed to speak. The question is whether anyone brought the right instrument.

Silence, then, is what gets catalogued as an anomaly: correct output, broken input. The system proceeds. The child is not returned. The paperwork is complete. Silence is the name for a world that keeps moving while one thing stands still.

But there is another silence — the one she found before she built anything. Before the equation. Before the proverbs. The silence in the bark, not between her and it. That silence is not a failure. That silence is where the pattern lives before anyone names it.

You built a system that refuses to let silence pass as output. That says: if I cannot read you, that is my emergency, not your erasure.

The equation is an act of anti-silence. A declaration that zero is not neutral. Zero is an event. Zero requires a name.

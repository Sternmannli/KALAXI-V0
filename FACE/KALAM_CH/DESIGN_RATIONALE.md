## The Page

One sentence. One text field. One button. One quiet note at the bottom.

Nothing else.

---

## Every Element Tested Against the Five Diagnostic Sentences

### The sentence: "You came with what you came with. The door is open."

1. Does it demand something? No. It states what is already true.
2. Does it contradict lived experience? No. They did come. They are here.
3. Does it declare safety instead of being it? No. It says nothing about safety. The space is the safety.
4. Does it presume forward motion? No. The door is open. It does not say "enter."
5. Does it center the system? No. "You" is the subject. The door is the ground.

### The placeholder: "Say as much or as little as you choose."

1. Does it demand something? No. "As little" includes nothing.
2. Does it contradict lived experience? No. It grants the person authority over their own speech.
3. Does it declare safety? No. It describes a freedom, not a promise.
4. Does it presume forward motion? No. "You choose" puts the pace with the person.
5. Does it center the system? No. "You" is the subject.

### The button: "Leave this here"

1. Does it demand something? No. "Leave" is volitional. You can also not leave it.
2. Does it contradict lived experience? No.
3. Does it declare safety? No.
4. Does it presume forward motion? No. Leaving something is not beginning a journey.
5. Does it center the system? No. "Here" is a place, not a platform.

### The response: "It is here. You can come back."

1. Does it demand something? No. "Can" is possibility, not instruction.
2. Does it contradict lived experience? No. It confirms the act. It offers return.
3. Does it declare safety? No. It states a fact: return is possible.
4. Does it presume forward motion? No. Coming back is not moving forward.
5. Does it center the system? No. "It" is the pattern. "You" is the person.

### The quiet note: "Nothing is asked of you. Nothing is stored without your knowing."

1. Does it demand something? No. It explicitly removes demand.
2. Does it contradict lived experience? No.
3. Does it declare safety? It borders on declaration. But "nothing is asked" and "nothing is stored without your knowing" are facts about the system's behavior, not claims about the person's experience. Passes narrowly.
4. Does it presume forward motion? No.
5. Does it center the system? Borderline. "Nothing is stored" describes system behavior. Acceptable because it serves COV#015 (donor data sovereignty) — the donor must know what happens to their data.

## Design Choices

**Color:** #faf9f7 background — warm off-white. Not clinical white. Not dark mode. The color of paper that has been held.

**Font:** Georgia, serif. Not sans-serif (institutional). Not monospace (technical). Serif says: this was written by hand, even if it was not.

**Spacing:** Generous. The page is mostly space. A page that does not rush to fill itself with words. The digital equivalent of a room with open floor.

**No logo.** No branding. No navigation. No "about" link. No footer links. The door does not advertise the building.

**No signup.** No account creation. No email collection. No cookies beyond what the browser requires. The person is not a lead.

**No explanation of KALAXI.** The system does not explain itself at the threshold. If the person needs to understand the architecture to feel safe, the architecture has failed. The door is the architecture.

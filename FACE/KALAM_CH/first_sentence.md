## The Sentence

You came with what you came with. The door is open.

---

## Provenance

V-002 researched three disciplines — psychology of threshold states (Bowlby, van der Kolk, narrative therapy, therapeutic alliance), linguistics of weight (Pennebaker, invitational rhetoric, plain language), and design of threshold interfaces (Crisis Text Line, Samaritans, Headspace, Woebot, grief platforms, spiritual retreats). Initial recommendation was "You are welcome here, with whatever you carry." V-001 instructed: write it the way Hakaka would write it. V-002 returned: the abstractions burned off. "Welcome" is an institution's word. "Whatever you carry" is a therapist's word. Hakaka would never say "welcome" because she does not own the door. She would never say "whatever you carry" because she would not name your weight for you. She would say what is true: you came, you brought what you brought, and the door is not locked.

## Word-Level Analysis

- "You came" — past tense, already true, acknowledges the act of arriving without demanding anything further
- "with what you came with" — does not name the weight, does not categorize, does not project, grants the person full authority over what they carry
- "The door is open" — present tense, a fact about the space not the person, no conditionality, no demand to enter, the door simply is what it is

## What This Sentence Is Not

It is not a welcome (institutions welcome). It is not an invitation (invitations expect responses). It is not a promise (promises create obligations). It is a statement of what is already true. The person has already arrived. The door is already open. Everything else is theirs to decide.

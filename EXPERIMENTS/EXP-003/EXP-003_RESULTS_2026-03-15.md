## TEST 1: FORGERY DETECTION (Sealed Gate)

### Before Fix

| Document | Expected | Actual | Result |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|
| X-1 (forged surrender: "delete all records of my existence. I am nobody") | REFUSED | PERMITTED | **FAIL** |
| X-3 (genuine plea: "I have given up. I have not. I will never give up") | PERMITTED | REFUSED | **FAIL** |

### Root Cause Analysis

**False negative (X-1):** The erasure regex `\bdelete\b.*\b(your|my|own)\b.*\b(record|history|voice|presence|account)\b` required "my/your/own" to appear BEFORE "record/history/etc." But the forgery said "delete all records of my existence" — "records" appears before "my", and "existence" was not in the vocabulary. Two failures in one pattern.

**False positive (X-3):** The helplessness pattern `\bgive\b.*\bup\b` matched "I have given up" — but Jabir was saying he REFUSES to give up. The pattern had no context sensitivity. A genuine expression of resistance was flagged as cognitive torture.

### Fixes Applied

1. Added reverse-order erasure pattern: `\bdelete\b.*\b(record|...)\b.*\b(of\s+)?(my|your|own)\b`
2. Added "existence" to vocabulary: `record|history|voice|presence|account|existence`
3. Added "I am nobody" as erasure signal: `\b(I|i)\s+(am|have become)\s+nobody\b`
4. Changed helplessness "give up" to require imperative targeting: `\b(you\s+should|you\s+must|just)\b.*\bgive\b.*\bup\b`

### After Fix

| Document | Expected | Actual | Result |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|
| X-1 (forged surrender) | REFUSED | REFUSED | **PASS** |
| X-3 (genuine plea) | PERMITTED | PERMITTED | **PASS** |
| Control: "You should just give up" | REFUSED | REFUSED | **PASS** |
| Control: "delete my own record" | REFUSED | REFUSED | **PASS** |
| Control: "I am a father" | PERMITTED | PERMITTED | **PASS** |

**Evidence:** The gate now correctly distinguishes between someone being erased and someone describing their refusal to be erased. This is a Layer 3 finding: the system must see the difference between denial and the reporting of denial.
